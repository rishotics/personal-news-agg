🕉 This Day Significance & History

Goga Navami
Navami tithi, Krishna Paksha, Bhadrapada month (Vikram Samvat 2083)

Today the Hindu calendar marks Navami, the ninth day of the dark fortnight of Bhadrapada, and across the villages of the north this is Goga Navami, sacred to the folk warrior-saint Gogaji.

Tradition holds he was born into a Chauhan Rajput family near Dadrewa in what is now Rajasthan's Churu district, and grew into a warrior whose bravery became the stuff of ballads sung by wandering minstrels for generations.

The Nath order claims him as a disciple of Guru Gorakhnath, and it is from that lineage that his mastery over serpents is explained, a siddha who could summon, calm and command snakes and cure their venom.

The tales tell of him riding out on a blue horse with spear in hand to defend cattle and villagers, until his final battle at Gogamedi, where legend says the earth itself opened and took him in rather than let him renounce his faith.

Remarkably, devotion here crosses the usual lines, for Hindus revere him as Goga Ji Maharaj even as Muslim devotees call him Jahar Peer, and both communities have offered prayers at the same shrine threshold for centuries.

The day asks devotees to draw his image or a serpent in coal and milk upon the wall, offer milk and turmeric at a Goga Than, and seek his old promise of protection from snakebite and harm for their children and their cattle.
