Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early, and a Tiny LoRA Fixes It

independent/academic · ['Zehao Jin', 'Ruixuan Deng', 'Junran Wang']
Why it matters
  1. Finds that thirteen pretrained base transformers reliably follow in-context references for only 1.4 to 3.6 lines despite having far more depth available.
  2. Shows extra pretrained compute loops add almost nothing, revealing models underuse their own depth for multi-step reference tracking rather than lacking capacity.
  3. A single rank-8 LoRA inserted at one early layer, with all original weights frozen, extends this reference-following computation substantially.
  4. Reports Qwen3-8B improves meaningfully on the tested task after this minimal intervention, demonstrating the fix is cheap and practical.
  5. Matters for anyone building reasoning or agentic LLMs since it suggests shallow effective depth, not architecture size, is silently capping long-context reasoning.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Ashwin Krishna Dashami – Dashami Shraddha (Pitru Paksha)
Krishna Dashami tithi, Krishna Paksha, Ashwin month (Vikram Samvat 2083), Pushya nakshatra

Today the moon stands on Krishna Paksha Dashami, the tenth day of the waning fortnight of Ashwin, with Pushya nakshatra overhead.

This Dashami falls inside Pitru Paksha, the sixteen-day stretch of the Hindu year set aside for shraddha, the rites that honour one's departed ancestors.

Tradition explains the origin of this fortnight through Karna, the great warrior of the Mahabharata who gave away gold and riches all his life but never once offered food or water to his forefathers.

When Karna died and reached heaven, he was astonished to find his plate heaped with jewels and gold instead of food.

Indra, king of the gods, told him he had received only what he had given, and granted him sixteen days back on earth to perform proper shraddha for his ancestors with rice, water and sesame seed, and from this grace Pitru Paksha was born.

Those who keep Dashami Shraddha today are asked to offer tarpan and pinda to their forebears, repaying in gratitude the debt owed to those who came before.
