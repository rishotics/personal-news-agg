🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Shukla Pratipada (Chandra Darshan)
Shukla Paksha Pratipada tithi (lasting until about 07:46 AM, after which Dwitiya begins), in the month of Bhadrapada under the Amanta reckoning, with Uttara Phalguni nakshatra, on a Saturday of Vikram Samvat 2083.

Long ago, as the Puranas tell it, the Moon-god Chandra wed all twenty-seven daughters of the sage-king Daksha, who are the nakshatras of the sky.

Chandra loved Rohini above the rest and neglected his other wives, so a furious Daksha cursed him to waste away night after night.

As the curse took hold, Chandra grew fainter and fainter until he vanished from the heavens entirely on the night of Amavasya.

The devas and sages, alarmed at losing the moon, begged Lord Shiva to intervene, and Shiva placed the crescent moon upon his own matted hair, softening the curse into a cycle of waxing and waning rather than a final end.

So each month, on the very first evening after Amavasya, the slender new crescent reappears in the western sky, and this reappearance is what devotees mark today as Chandra Darshan.

The day asks people to step outside at dusk, seek the first sliver of the moon, and offer it a quiet arghya of water and gratitude for renewal after darkness.
