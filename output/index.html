🕉 This Day Significance & History

Shukla Paksha Trayodashi (Shani Pradosh Vrat)
Shukla Paksha Trayodashi, Ashwin month, Saturday — tithi runs until early afternoon before Chaturdashi begins

Today's panchang names the Shukla Paksha Trayodashi of the Ashwin month, and because this thirteenth tithi has landed on a Saturday, tradition calls it Shani Pradosh.

The old katha behind every Trayodashi evening goes back to Chandra, the moon god, who wed all twenty-seven daughters of the sage-king Daksha but gave his heart only to Rohini, leaving her sisters neglected.

The spurned wives complained to their father, and when Chandra ignored Daksha's counsel to treat them equally, the enraged king cursed him with a wasting affliction that began to dim his light over the world.

Desperate, the gods turned to Brahma, who told Chandra that only Shiva could help, and the moon god undertook six months of fierce penance before a Shiva lingam.

Pleased, Shiva appeared as a Jyotirlinga at twilight on this very tithi and softened the curse, decreeing that Chandra would wane for a fortnight and wax for the next rather than vanish forever, which is why devotees still hold the pradosh kaal just after sunset as Shiva's most approachable hour.

Because this year's Trayodashi falls on a Shani-ruled Saturday, the day asks devotees to fast until dusk and pray to both Shiva and Shani together, seeking relief from hardship, discipline for the mind, and the same mercy that once spared the moon.
