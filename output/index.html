🕉 This Day Significance & History

Ashwin Krishna Paksha Dwitiya (within Pitru Paksha)
Dwitiya tithi, Krishna Paksha, Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083 (Nakshatra: Revati)

Today falls on the second day of the dark fortnight of Ashwin, inside the sixteen-day window that Hindus call Pitru Paksha, the fortnight set aside for the ancestors.

Tradition traces the origin of this observance to the Mahabharata warrior Karna, who in life gave away gold, jewels and land to anyone who asked but never once offered food or water in the name of his forefathers.

When Karna died and reached the heavens, he was served only gold and precious stones to eat, for that alone was what he had given away on earth.

Bewildered, he pleaded with Indra, who explained that a soul receives in the afterlife only what it once offered to its own ancestors.

Moved by his ignorance rather than his intent, the gods granted Karna a fixed number of days to return to earth and perform proper shraddha, offering food, water and sesame to his pitrs.

That grace period became Pitru Paksha itself, and on each of its tithis, including today's Dwitiya, families continue to offer tarpan and pind daan to the ancestor who departed on that same lunar day, asking only that gratitude be repaid before the fortnight closes on Sarvapitri Amavasya.
