Independent/Academic (interpretability research) · ['Pavel Tikhonov', 'Anton Korznikov', 'Matvey Mikhalchuk', 'Nikita Dragunov', 'Temurbek Rahmatullaev']
Why it matters
  1. Shows LLMs exhibit a previously undocumented linearity: linearly combining inputs from distinct text streams yields a superposition of the individual next-token distributions
  2. Challenges the standard assumption that transformers are fundamentally non-linear black boxes despite attention and MLP nonlinearities
  3. The finding is demonstrated empirically across multiple models, giving a concrete, testable mechanistic claim rather than a speculative theory
  4. Matters to interpretability researchers at Anthropic, DeepMind, and academia who rely on linear representation hypotheses to reverse-engineer model internals
  5. Opens new directions for efficient batched inference, prompt composition, and safety analysis if model computation can be decomposed linearly across contexts

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Purnima (Satyanarayana Vrat)
Shukla Paksha Purnima, Bhadrapada month, Purva Bhadrapada Nakshatra, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon stands full and the tithi is Purnima, the fifteenth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, a day tradition sets aside for the worship of Lord Satyanarayana, an approachable form of Vishnu, and for reverence to Chandra, the moon god.

The story goes that once, in the forest hermitage of Naimisharanya, the sage Suta was asked by the assembled rishis how ordinary householders might find ease from worldly suffering, and he answered by recounting the Satyanarayana Katha that Narada had once heard from Vishnu himself.

In the katha, a poor woodcutter and a struggling trader are shown mercy after they perform the simple vrat of the full moon, offering prasad of banana, wheat, milk and ghee, and sharing it among family and neighbours.

Later in the same telling, a proud king named Tungadhwaj scorns the offering and refuses to eat it as beneath his station, only to lose his kingdom and his sons before humbling himself and returning to the vrat.

The tale closes with prosperity restored to all who kept faith and hardship falling upon those who dismissed the small, communal act of devotion out of pride.

The day asks the same of those who observe it now: to fast till evening, to gather for a simple puja at moonrise, and to share the prasad rather than eat it alone.
