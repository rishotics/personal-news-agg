🤖 Paper of the Day AI & Robotics Research

LLM Theory

Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early, and a Tiny LoRA Fixes It

academic (independent/university) · ['Zehao Jin', 'Ruixuan Deng', 'Junran Wang']
Why it matters
  1. Identifies that pretrained transformers only use a tiny fraction of their depth to follow references in context, reliably tracking just 1.4 to 3.6 lines across thirteen base models.
  2. Shows adding pretrained recurrent loops barely helps, challenging the assumption that more compute depth automatically improves reasoning chains.
  3. Fix is remarkably cheap: a single rank-8 LoRA trained at one early layer, with all original model weights kept frozen.
  4. Demonstrated concretely on Qwen3-8B, extending the model's effective reference-following computation with minimal added parameters.
  5. Matters for anyone building long-context or multi-step reasoning systems, since it pinpoints a previously hidden depth-utilization bottleneck and a practical low-cost remedy.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Krishna Paksha Dashami — Pitru Paksha Shraddha
Krishna Paksha Dashami tithi, Ashwin month, Pushya Nakshatra, Vikram Samvat 2083 (Monday)

Today the moon moves through Krishna Paksha Dashami of Ashwin, placing the day within Pitru Paksha, the fortnight when Hindus turn their attention to the souls of their ancestors.

Tradition traces the origin of this fortnight to the warrior Karna of the Mahabharata, who had spent his life giving away gold and jewels to anyone who asked of him.

When Karna died and his soul reached heaven, he was astonished to find that the food placed before him turned to gold instead of nourishment.

Questioning Indra about this strange fate, Karna was told that he had donated gold his entire life but had never once offered food or water to his own ancestors.

Karna explained that he had been unaware of his true lineage, and so was granted leave to return to earth for a fortnight to perform shraddha and feed the hungry in his forefathers' names.

That fortnight became Pitru Paksha, and the day asks the living to pour water and offer food in memory of those who came before them, repaying a debt that is owed quietly but never forgotten.
