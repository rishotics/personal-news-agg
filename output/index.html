🕉 This Day Significance & History

Sharad Purnima (Kojagari Purnima)
Shukla Paksha Chaturdashi till late morning, then Purnima begins, in the Hindu month of Ashwin (Ashwina/Asho), Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon turns full in the month of Ashwin, and this night is kept as Sharad Purnima, also called Kojagari Purnima, said to be the brightest full moon of the year.

Tradition holds that once the monsoon clouds withdraw, the moon's light falls on earth like amrit, nectar, so families set out bowls of kheer beneath the open sky to receive it.

At midnight, it is said, Goddess Lakshmi descends and moves from house to house across the world asking one question, "Ko jagarti?" meaning "Who is awake?"

Those she finds still awake, lamps lit and her name on their lips, are the ones she blesses with wealth, fortune, and well-being.

In the Braj region the same moonlit night is remembered differently, as the night of Krishna's Maha Raas, his dance with the gopis under that same full moon.

The day asks only this of those who observe it: stay wakeful through the night in devotion, rather than let the goddess pass by an unanswered door.
