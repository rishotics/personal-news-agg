🕉 This Day Significance & History

Shukla Paksha Trayodashi (Pradosh Vrat)
Trayodashi tithi of Shukla Paksha in the Hindu month of Ashwin (Vikram Samvat 2083), turning to Chaturdashi by early afternoon.

Today the moon stands on Shukla Trayodashi, the thirteenth day of its waxing fortnight in the month of Ashwin, before slipping into Chaturdashi by the early afternoon.

Every Trayodashi, whether in the bright or dark fortnight, belongs to the Pradosh Vrat, a fast kept in the fragile hour of twilight for Lord Shiva and his consort Parvati.

The story behind it reaches back to the Samudra Manthan, when gods and demons churned the cosmic ocean for the nectar of immortality and instead loosed Halahala, a poison fierce enough to consume all creation.

Panicked, the devas and asuras turned to Shiva, who swallowed the poison to save the universe, and Parvati, fearing it would travel further, held his throat shut, turning it blue and giving him the name Neelkanth.

Moved by this act of sacrifice, the gods are said to have gathered before him at dusk in gratitude, which is why the Pradosh puja is still timed to that same meeting point of day and night.

The day asks its observers to fast until evening, light a lamp for Shiva and Parvati at twilight, and sit for a moment with the thought that restraint, even to the point of self-sacrifice, is what holds the world together.
