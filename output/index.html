🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Shukla Dwitiya
Shukla Paksha Dwitiya (second tithi) of Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083, Nakshatra Hasta.

Today is Dwitiya, the second day of the bright, waxing fortnight of Bhadrapada, the tithi that follows the sighting of the first slender crescent after Amavasya.

Tradition traces this crescent moon to the sage Daksha, who gave his twenty-seven star-daughters, the Nakshatras, in marriage to Chandra, the moon god.

Chandra showered his love on only one wife, Rohini, and ignored the rest, so the neglected sisters complained bitterly to their father.

Enraged, Daksha cursed Chandra to wither away and lose his light forever, and the moon began to fade night after night.

Chandra fled to Mount Kailash and begged forgiveness from Lord Shiva, who took pity, set the crescent moon upon his own matted locks, and eased the curse so that Chandra would only wane and wax each month instead of vanishing for good.

That is why the days just after the new moon call for a glance at the returning crescent low in the sky and a quiet word of thanks that light, once lost, still finds its way back.
