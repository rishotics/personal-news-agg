🤖 Paper of the Day AI & Robotics Research

Agents

LEASH: Uncertainty-Aware Weak-to-Strong Escalation and Oversight for Computer-Use Agents

academic consortium (UW-Madison / collaborators) · Qingni Wang, Changdae Oh, Yuzhe Yang, Sharon Li, Jiachen Yang
Why it matters
  1. Introduces LEASH, a weak-to-strong escalation framework that lets a cheap open-weight agent run by default and only calls a frontier model when uncertain
  2. Tackles the real production problem of computer-use agents where invoking frontier models at every step is prohibitively expensive
  3. Uses uncertainty-aware triggers to decide escalation, offering a principled alternative to naive always-strong or always-weak deployment
  4. Directly affects companies building GUI and computer-use agents who must balance cost against long-horizon task reliability
  5. Points toward a practical deployment pattern that could become standard for cost-efficient agentic systems going forward

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Maha Saptami
Shukla Paksha Saptami (seventh tithi, waxing fortnight), Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the tithi turns to Shukla Saptami, the seventh day of the bright fortnight of Ashwin, and in homes and pandals across eastern India it is observed as Maha Saptami, the formal opening of Durga Puja worship.

Before sunrise, priests take the Nabapatrika, a bundle of nine plants bound together with banana at its heart, down to the river or pond for a ceremonial bath, after which it is wrapped in a red-bordered white sari and set beside the clay image of Ganesha.

Devotees fondly call this bundle Kola Bou, the banana bride, though in truth she represents nine forms of the Goddess residing within nine different plants rather than any wife of Ganesha.

It is on this morning that the priest performs Pran Pratistha, the chanting of mantras that is believed to call the Goddess's life force into her clay form, so that the image standing in the pandal is no longer mere earth but Durga herself, present and awake.

The night carries its own rites too, as pandits recite the Durga Saptashati from the Markandeya Purana, retelling how the gods poured their combined radiance into Durga so she could ride out against the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

What the day asks of those who keep it is simple reverence, to welcome the Goddess with flowers, lamps, and song as the three central days of the festival begin, trusting that her strength will see them through to the Goddess's triumph on Vijaya Dashami.
