🤖 Paper of the Day AI & Robotics Research

LLM

Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs

Independent/Academic (interpretability research) · Pavel Tikhonov, Anton Korznikov, Matvey Mikhalchuk, Nikita Dragunov, Temurbek Rahmatullaev
Why it matters
  1. Shows that linearly combining inputs from distinct text streams makes LLMs output a superposition of both next-token distributions, dubbed the Superposition of Distributions property.
  2. This reveals unexpected linear structure hidden beneath the highly non-linear attention and MLP layers that define modern transformers.
  3. The finding is demonstrated empirically across multiple LLM architectures, giving concrete evidence rather than a purely theoretical claim.
  4. It matters for anyone building or interpreting LLMs, since it exposes a computational primitive relevant to safety, steering, and mechanistic interpretability work.
  5. If linear superposition is a general property, it could reshape how researchers design interpretability tools, activation steering methods, and prompt-composition techniques going forward.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Krishna Paksha Pratipada — Pitru Paksha Begins
Krishna Paksha Pratipada tithi, Ashwina month, Vikram Samvat 2083, Sunday

Today the moon enters its waning fortnight as Krishna Paksha Pratipada, and with it begins the solemn sixteen-day observance known as Pitru Paksha, dedicated to the souls of one's ancestors.

Tradition traces its origin to the Mahabharata, when the great warrior Karna, celebrated in life for his boundless charity of gold and jewels, arrived in the afterlife only to find gold and jewels served to him instead of food.

Bewildered, Karna asked the reason, and he was told that he had given away wealth all his life but had never once offered food or water to his ancestors in shraddha, for he had never known who they were.

Moved by his plea of ignorance, he was granted leave to return to the earth for a period of days so that he could finally perform the proper rites and donate food and water in their memory.

That span of days granted to Karna is remembered still as Pitru Paksha, when it is said the souls of the departed draw near the earthly realm awaiting remembrance from their descendants.

The day asks of those who observe it to pause amid ordinary business, offer tarpana and food in the name of one's forebears, and repay in humility a debt that wealth alone can never settle.
