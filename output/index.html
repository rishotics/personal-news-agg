🕉 This Day Significance & History

Hal Shashthi (Balaram Jayanti)
Krishna Paksha Shashthi tithi, Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon stands on Shashthi tithi of Krishna Paksha in the month of Bhadrapada, and North Indian households call it Hal Shashthi, also known as Halchhath or Lalahi Chhath.

The day is kept in honour of Balarama, Krishna's elder brother born to Rohini, whose emblem was the plough and the pestle and who was revered as the protector of farmers and the tiller's soil.

Mothers across Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and the Braj region rise before dawn, dig a small pond of earth in the courtyard, and plant branches of palash and ber beside it before beginning their fast.

Through the day they eat nothing that has been touched by a plough, taking instead only rice and greens that grew wild, in tribute to Balarama's simple, agrarian nature.

The vrat katha most often told on this day is of a milkmaid who sold adulterated milk out of greed and saw her own child gravely harmed for it, a warning passed down to keep the fast pure and honest.

The day asks mothers to keep their vow with the same steadfastness Balarama showed in guarding those he loved, praying it brings long life and health to their children.
