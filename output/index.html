🕉 This Day Significance & History

Shukla Trayodashi – Pradosh Vrat
Trayodashi tithi, Shukla Paksha, Bhadrapada month (Vikram Samvat 2083)

Today the moon waxes toward fullness on the thirteenth day of Bhadrapada's bright fortnight, a tithi the tradition sets aside for Lord Shiva as the Pradosh Vrat.

The story goes back to the churning of the cosmic ocean, when gods and demons together turned Mount Mandara to draw out the nectar of immortality.

From the churning arose Halahala, a poison so deadly that it threatened to consume all creation, and the terrified devas ran to Shiva for refuge.

Out of compassion for the universe, Shiva drank the poison himself, and Parvati, moved by devotion, held his throat so the venom could travel no further, leaving it stained blue and giving him the name Neelkanth.

It is said this act of cosmic mercy unfolded in the twilight hours of a Trayodashi, which is why that fleeting window between day and night, the pradosh kaal, is still kept sacred to him.

Devotees who observe the vrat today fast through the day, bathe before sunset, and gather in the day's fading light to offer bael leaves and chant to Shiva and Parvati, asking, as the legend teaches, for release from old sins and protection from poison of every kind.
