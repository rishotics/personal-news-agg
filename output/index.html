🕉 This Day Significance & History

Durga Ashtami (Maha Ashtami)
Shukla Paksha Ashtami tithi (begins after Saptami ends near sunrise), Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083, with Purva Ashadha nakshatra.

Today is Durga Ashtami, the eighth day of Sharad Navratri, when the moon rides the Shukla Paksha Ashtami tithi of the Ashwin month and the great Durga Puja reaches its peak.

Tradition tells that while Goddess Durga was locked in fierce combat with the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, his generals Chanda and Munda tried to strike at her from behind, and the treachery made her face darken with rage.

From the goddess's own forehead then burst forth a terrifying form with blood-red eyes and a lolling tongue, and it was she, named Chamunda, who annihilated Chanda and Munda in that instant.

So sacred is this exact moment that worship is timed to the breath between two tithis, the last twenty-four minutes of Ashtami fused with the first twenty-four of Navami, a forty-eight-minute Sandhi Kaal in which devotees light 108 lamps and offer 108 lotuses to the goddess.

On this same day across many homes and pandals, young unmarried girls are seated and worshipped as living Kumaris, the goddess made visible in human form.

The day asks its observers to see that even righteous fury must be aimed true, and to honor the living presence of the divine feminine in the women and girls before them.
