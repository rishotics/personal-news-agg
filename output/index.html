EyeRobot 2.0: Active Gaze for Precise Manipulation without Wrist Cameras

UC Berkeley · Kush Hari, Justin Kerr, Nidhya Shivakumar, Samarth Mahapatra, Carmelo Sferrazza
Why it matters
  1. Introduces active robotic gaze that swivels two eye cameras to fixate a 3D point, replacing wrist-mounted cameras entirely

  3. Removes the need for wrist cameras, a major simplification for hardware design and sim-to-real transfer in dexterous manipulation

  5. Signals a shift toward active perception strategies as a core robot design axis rather than static multi-camera rigs

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Indira Ekadashi
Ekadashi tithi, Krishna Paksha, Ashwin month (Vikram Samvat 2083)

Today falls on the eleventh lunar day of the dark fortnight of Ashwin, known as Indira Ekadashi, a day sacred to Lord Vishnu and bound up with the memory of one's ancestors.

Tradition holds that King Indrasena, a devout and powerful ruler of Mahishmati, once received the sage Narada in his court.

Narada told the king that on a journey to Yamaloka he had seen Indrasena's own father suffering there, unable to attain liberation because he had broken an Ekadashi fast midway through in a former life.

The father had sent word through Narada asking his son to keep the Indira Ekadashi vrat with full devotion and offer its merit to him so he might be freed.

Indrasena fasted exactly as instructed, performed shraddha and gave alms, and when he broke the fast flowers are said to have fallen from the sky as his father's soul rose to Vishnu's abode of Vaikuntha.

The day therefore asks observers to fast with sincerity, to remember their departed elders, and to offer whatever merit they earn toward the peace of those who came before them.
