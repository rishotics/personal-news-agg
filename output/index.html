🤖 Paper of the Day AI & Robotics Research

LLM

Graph Machine: Towards Better Pretraining via Edges

independent/academic · ['Lintai Hou']
Why it matters
  1. Introduces the Graph Machine, a new architecture using O(n)-sized state accessed via sparse, dynamic routing rather than fixed-size or static-sparse memory.
  2. Novelty lies in combining dynamic edge-based routing with linear state size, avoiding the tradeoff between full attention cost and fixed-state compression seen in prior efficient architectures.
  3. Targets a core scaling bottleneck in transformer pretraining, offering a credible alternative path to reduce quadratic attention costs while preserving unrestricted accessibility.
  4. Matters most to researchers and labs building next-generation efficient LLM backbones seeking alternatives to standard attention or SSM-style fixed states.
  5. If validated at scale, this could shift pretraining architecture design away from both dense attention and rigid state-space models toward dynamic sparse routing schemes.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Pithori Amavasya
Krishna Chaturdashi at sunrise giving way to Amavasya, Krishna Paksha, Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083

The dark fortnight of Bhadrapada ends today as the tithi turns from Chaturdashi into Amavasya, the no-moon day that this year carries the name Pithori Amavasya.

Tradition says the vrat's story was first told by the goddess Parvati to Indrani, the queen of Indra, so that mortal mothers would have her counsel in their hour of grief.

In that telling a woman had lost child after child soon after birth, and in her hopelessness she was guided to worship the sixty-four Yoginis on this very Amavasya of Bhadrapada.

She raised no costly idol for the goddess, only small figures kneaded from ordinary flour, pith, and simple food cooked in her own kitchen, offered with complete faith.

Her devotion was answered, for her children lived long and well thereafter, and the day itself took its name from that humble flour offering.

So even now mothers across the country shape the Devi and the sixty-four Yoginis from wheat or rice flour, keep a fast through the day, and ask nothing more than health and long life for their children.
