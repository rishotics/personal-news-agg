The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

Shanghai AI Laboratory · Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu
Why it matters
  1. Conducts a causal audit of the popular thinking-with-images paradigm where MLLMs actively crop, zoom, and manipulate images during reasoning
  2. Finds that active visual tool-use often yields only marginal or negative accuracy gains over simple direct inference despite much higher token cost
  3. Shows models frequently crop irrelevant regions yet still answer correctly, revealing the tool calls are often causally disconnected from the final answer
  4. Challenges a widely adopted design pattern used across recent multimodal agent and reasoning systems built on visual tool invocation
  5. Suggests the field's benchmarks for tool-augmented visual reasoning may be measuring illusory rather than genuine causal capability, demanding better evaluation protocols
