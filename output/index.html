🕉 This Day Significance & History

Ashvina Krishna Paksha Dwitiya (Pitru Paksha)
Krishna Paksha Dwitiya tithi in the Hindu month of Ashvina, Nakshatra Revati, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today is the second day of the dark fortnight of Ashvina, placing it inside Pitru Paksha, the sixteen-day period the Hindu calendar sets aside each year for honouring one's ancestors.

Tradition traces the origin of this fortnight to Karna, the great warrior of the Mahabharata, who after dying in the Kurukshetra war ascended to the heavenly realm.

There he was astonished to find that whenever he reached for food, he was offered only gold and jewels instead, for as the story explains, he had donated wealth all his life but had never offered food or water to his own ancestors.

Realising his omission, Karna begged to be sent back to earth, and he was granted a fortnight in which to perform the shraddha rites and feed the poor in his forefathers' memory.

That period of return became fixed on the calendar as Pitru Paksha, observed every year in this same waning fortnight of the lunar month.

The day therefore asks householders to pause, remember the elders who came before them, and mark the occasion with a simple offering of food, water, or a quiet prayer rather than any grand display.
