🕉 This Day Significance & History

Durga Ashtami / Maha Navami (Ashwina Shukla Ashtami-Navami)
Shukla Paksha Ashtami tithi (till about 10:51 AM, then Navami begins), Ashwina month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the waxing moon of Ashwina carries the eighth tithi into the ninth, the hinge on which Durga Puja turns from Ashtami to Maha Navami.

Tradition holds that the warrior goddess Durga, created by the combined radiance of the gods to destroy what none of them alone could defeat, met the shape-shifting buffalo demon Mahishasura in a battle that raged for nine nights.

On the eighth night she is said to have taken the fierce form of Chamunda to cut down the demons Chanda and Munda, a moment many households and pandals mark with the brief, intense Sandhi Puja at the exact meeting point of Ashtami and Navami.

As dawn brings Navami, the legend holds that Durga finally corners Mahishasura and ends his terror, completing the victory that Vijayadashami will celebrate the next day.

In Bengal and across much of India this stretch of days is kept as Durga Puja, with the goddess worshipped in her many forms alongside rites of Kanya Puja, honouring young girls as living embodiments of her shakti.

The day asks devotees to recognise that courage and protection, like the goddess herself, must sometimes take a terrible form before peace can return.
