CUHK · Kun Li, Zexuan Qiu, Tianhua Zhang, Irwin King, Helen Meng
Why it matters

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Amavasya (Pithori Amavasya)
Amavasya tithi, Krishna Paksha, Bhadrapada month (Vikram Samvat 2083); Purva Phalguni nakshatra.

Today the moon disappears entirely, marking Amavasya at the end of Krishna Paksha in the month of Bhadrapada, a day known in many households as Pithori Amavasya.

Tradition tells of a grieving mother whose children died one after another, leaving her hollow with sorrow and without answers.

The Divine Mother, moved by her suffering, appeared before her and instructed her to fashion sixty-four small images from flour representing the sixty-four Yoginis and to worship them with a full heart.

The woman obeyed, fasting through the day and offering her devotion without reservation, and in time her children were restored to her, safe and well.

Since then, married women across parts of India repeat her vow every Bhadrapada Amavasya, kneading rice or wheat flour into figures of the goddesses and laying a blessing hand on their children's heads.

The day asks householders to turn toward their family's wellbeing, to remember those who came before them in the year's quiet dark, and to trust that humble devotion is never wasted.
