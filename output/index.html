Fingers as Legs: Learning Self-Supported Locomotion and Manipulation with an Anthropomorphic Hand

ETH Zurich · ['Amirhossein Kazemipour', 'Hehui Zheng', 'Robert Katzschmann']
Why it matters
  1. Trains a single anthropomorphic hand to walk on its own fingers, self-supporting its full body weight while retaining its original manipulation design.
  2. Novel because the same finger joints and position controller used for grasping are repurposed unmodified for locomotion, unifying two skills in one morphology.
  3. Onboard power and compute make the platform fully self-contained, a credible systems result beyond simulation-only demonstrations common in this space.
  4. Matters to roboticists exploring multi-functional limbs, since it challenges the assumption that legs and manipulators must be separate specialized appendages.
  5. Points toward future robots that reduce actuator count and mechanical complexity by learning to reuse manipulation hardware for mobility on demand.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Shukla Paksha Saptami (Bhadrapada)
Shukla Paksha Saptami tithi, Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon completes its seventh waxing day, Shukla Saptami, in the month of Bhadrapada, and tradition turns this tithi over to Surya, the Sun God.

Every Saptami of the lunar calendar is held sacred to Surya, whose blazing chariot is drawn across the sky by seven horses said to represent the seven colours of light.

The story is told of the sage Kashyapa and his wife Aditi, whose long tapasya was rewarded with a son of such radiance that he became Surya, sent to dispel darkness and ignorance from the three worlds.

Surya's charioteer is Aruna, a figure born incomplete from his mother Vinata's impatience, who nonetheless rose each dawn in unwavering service and earned his place at the front of the sun's chariot.

Devotees who honour Saptami rise before sunrise, offer arghya of water facing the sun, and recite hymns such as the Aditya Hridayam seeking health, clarity and inner strength.

The day asks only that one greet the rising sun with humility, treating it not as a mere star but as a living witness to truth and disciplined effort.

It is widely regarded as the opening act of Japanese militarism in Asia and a crucial step on the path to World War II, an event many in China mark as the war's true beginning.
