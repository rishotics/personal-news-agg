🕉 This Day Significance & History

Ganesh Chaturthi
Shukla Paksha Chaturthi tithi (begins after Tritiya ends near dawn), Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Tradition holds that Goddess Parvati, wishing for a doorkeeper of her own who would obey only her, fashioned a boy out of turmeric and sandalwood paste and breathed life into him.

She set the child to guard her bathing chamber, and when Lord Shiva returned home and found his path blocked by an unfamiliar boy, an angry quarrel broke out between them.

In the ensuing clash Shiva severed the boy's head, and when Parvati's grief and fury threatened to unmake creation, Shiva sent his attendants to bring the head of the first creature they found, which was an elephant.

Shiva placed the elephant's head upon the boy's body and restored him to life, naming him Ganesha and declaring that he would be worshipped before all other gods at the start of every undertaking.

On this day devotees install clay images of Ganesha in homes and public pandals, offering modaks, durva grass, and red flowers as the ten-day Ganeshotsav begins.

The day asks worshippers to seek Ganesha's blessing as Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, before beginning any new work, journey, or venture.
