Independent/Academic (Robotics Interpretability) · Bryce Grant, Xijia Zhao, Peng Wang
Why it matters

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Papankusha Ekadashi
Shukla Paksha, Ekadashi tithi, month of Ashwina

Today the Hindu calendar reads Shukla Paksha Ekadashi in the month of Ashwina, two days after the close of Dussehra, and it is known as Papankusha Ekadashi.

The scriptures frame this day as a dialogue in which King Yudhishthira asks Krishna to name and explain the Ekadashi that falls in the bright fortnight of Ashwina, and Krishna answers that he will reveal its glories.

Krishna tells the king that Oh king, please listen as I explain the glories of this Ekadasi- Papankusha Ekadasi - which removes all sins, however grave or long accumulated they may be.

He adds that offering one's humble obeisances unto Lord Vishnu, the rider of Garuda, one can achieve the same merit as is gained by performing great penances for a long time restraining and controlling the senses.

In keeping with this teaching, it is dedicated to Lord Vishnu, and the tradition is to fast, pray and keep a quieter day after the noise of the festival.

The day asks devotees to keep a simple fast, chant the names of Vishnu, and let the discipline of the vrata act like a goad that reins in the restless mind.
