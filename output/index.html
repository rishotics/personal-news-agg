🕉 This Day Significance & History

Mahalaya Amavasya (Sarva Pitru Amavasya)
Krishna Paksha Amavasya in the Hindu month of Ashwin, Vikram Samvat 2083.

Today is Amavasya, the moonless night that closes Pitru Paksha, the fortnight set aside for the ancestors, and it is known as Mahalaya, the great dissolution, when every forgotten soul is remembered at once.

Tradition holds that during these sixteen days the pitrs, the departed fathers and mothers of a family, descend from their resting place to hover near their descendants, waiting to be fed and acknowledged.

The story told to explain this custom goes back to the Mahabharata, where the warrior Karna, famed in life for giving away gold without limit, dies and finds only jewels and riches to eat in the afterlife because he had never once offered food or water to his own forefathers.

Told of his error, Karna is sent back to earth for sixteen days to perform the rites he had neglected, pouring out tarpan and pinda to his ancestors, and this reprieve becomes the origin of Pitru Paksha itself.

On this final Amavasya families gather at riverbanks and home shrines to offer sesame seeds and water for every ancestor, named or unnamed, and to feed crows and cows who are believed to carry the offering onward.

The day asks the living to pause and settle their debt of gratitude to those who came before, for tomorrow the goddess Durga begins her descent and the fortnight of mourning gives way to the fortnight of celebration.
