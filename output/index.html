🕉 This Day Significance & History

Maha Saptami
Shukla Paksha Saptami tithi, Hindu month of Ashwin, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today is Maha Saptami, the seventh day of the bright fortnight of Ashwin, when the Durga Puja moves from invocation into its most intense phase of worship.

Before sunrise priests bind together nine sacred plants, including banana, turmeric, pomegranate, and paddy, into a single bundle called the Nabapatrika, said to hold the nine forms of the Goddess.

This bundle, dressed in a red-bordered sari and popularly called Kola Bou, is carried to a river or pond for a ceremonial bath that marks the purification of the earth itself.

Afterward the bathed Nabapatrika is installed beside Lord Ganesha in the pandal, and the Goddess is formally invoked and bathed through the Mahasnan ritual using a mirror to reflect her image.

Tradition holds that from this moment Durga, born of the combined power of the gods, begins to stir toward her coming battle against the buffalo demon Mahishasura, whom no man or god could slay alone.

The day asks worshippers to see Shakti living even in the leaves and roots of the earth, and to carry that same courage forward as the Puja builds toward Ashtami and Navami.
