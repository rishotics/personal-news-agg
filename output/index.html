🕉 This Day Significance & History

Vamana Jayanti (Bhadrapada Shukla Dwadashi)
Shukla Paksha Dwadashi tithi in the Hindu month of Bhadrapada, Shravana Nakshatra, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the Hindu calendar stands on the twelfth tithi of Bhadrapada's bright fortnight, Shukla Paksha Dwadashi, remembered across many traditions as Vamana Jayanti.

Tradition holds that the demon-king Bali, grandson of the devoted Prahlada, had grown so mighty through his sacrifices that he had defeated Indra and taken the three worlds for himself.

To curb this pride without destroying so noble a devotee, Vishnu came before Bali's sacrificial fire as Vamana, a small Brahmin boy carrying nothing more than an umbrella and a water-pot.

When the boy asked for only three paces of land, Bali agreed despite his own guru Shukra's warning that this visitor was no ordinary mendicant, and Vamana then swelled into his cosmic Trivikrama form, covering the whole earth with one stride and the entire sky with the next.

Finding no ground left for the promised third step, Bali offered his own head, and Vishnu, moved by this surrender, placed his foot there and sent him to rule the netherworld in honour rather than disgrace.

The day therefore asks its observers to fast, recall the tale, and choose the same quiet humility that let a great king keep his word even at the cost of his crown.
