🕉 This Day Significance & History

Krishna Paksha Panchami
Krishna Paksha Panchami tithi (until 7:24 PM, then Shashthi begins), Kartik month by the North Indian purnimanta reckoning (Ashwin in the amanta calendar), Vikram Samvat 2083, Mrigashira nakshatra.

The panchang for October 30, 2026 places the day in Krishna Paksha Panchami, the fifth tithi of the waning moon, which runs until 7:24 PM before giving way to Shashthi.

No major named festival or vrat falls today, and the panchang for this date lists no major observance, so the day's meaning rests on the character of the tithi itself.

In Hindu tradition every Panchami, whether in the bright or dark fortnight, is held to belong to the Nagas, the serpent deities, since the fifth tithi of each month is traditionally reserved for their worship.

The story behind this reverence is drawn from the Mahabharata, where King Janamejaya, avenging his father Parikshit's death from the serpent king Takshaka's bite, undertook a great sarpa yajna meant to destroy every snake upon the earth.

As serpents by the thousand fell into the sacrificial fire, it was on a Panchami tithi that the king was persuaded to halt the yajna, and the serpent race, including Takshaka, was granted a new lease of life.

The day therefore asks those who observe it to honour the Nagas as guardians of the earth's hidden forces, offering milk and quiet prayer at a serpent shrine rather than harm, and to seek protection from the fear that a snake's bite represents.
