🕉 This Day Significance & History

Pithori Amavasya
Krishna Chaturdashi tithi giving way to Amavasya, Krishna Paksha, Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today's new moon, arriving at the close of Bhadrapada's dark fortnight, is kept by many households as Pithori Amavasya, a day devoted to the worship of Goddess Durga and the sixty-four Yoginis for the sake of one's children.

Tradition holds that Goddess Parvati herself first spoke this vrat to Indrani, wife of Indra, so that mothers everywhere could shield their children from harm.

The katha remembered on this day tells of a daughter-in-law named Videha, who had lost her children and was told to follow a secret rite, hiding among bel leaves and waiting through the night for the sixty-four Yoginis to appear.

When she poured out her grief before them at midnight, the Yoginis were moved, and her sons came back to life and returned with her to a household transformed by joy.

In her memory, mothers today fast from sunrise, shape tiny figures of the Yoginis and Devi from wheat or rice flour, and offer them kheer, fruit and sweets before breaking their fast in the evening.

The day asks every mother to renew her watch over her children's health and long life, and to remember, as Videha once did, that devotion can turn sorrow into homecoming.
