🕉 This Day Significance & History

Masik Karthigai (Krittika Nakshatra Vrat)
Krishna Paksha Pratipada, Kartika month, with the Moon entering Krittika Nakshatra by evening

Today the lunar day is Krishna Paksha Pratipada, the first day of the waning fortnight as the holy month of Kartika opens, and by evening the Moon moves into Krittika Nakshatra, making this a Masik Karthigai day.

Tradition holds that Lord Vishnu and Lord Brahma once argued over which of them was supreme, and Lord Shiva appeared as an infinite column of fire to settle a dispute between Lord Vishnu and Lord Brahma.

Vishnu dove downward as a boar and Brahma flew upward as a swan, yet neither could find the base or the crown of that blazing pillar, and both conceded that Shiva's light could not be measured.

The same fire is remembered as the source of Kartikeya, for on this day devotees light rows of oil lamps in homes and temples to honour Lord Shiva's cosmic flame and the birth of Lord Murugan from six divine sparks.

Those six sparks, legend says, were nursed by six celestial mothers, the Krittikas, until the infant god merged into a single form with six faces.

What the day asks is simple: light a lamp at dusk without distraction, and let its small flame stand, however briefly, for a fire too vast to measure.

On This Day in History
1962
Known as Black Saturday, a Soviet surface-to-air missile shot down an American U-2 over Cuba, killing its pilot Major Rudolf Anderson in the only combat death of the Cuban Missile Crisis, even as a Soviet submarine crew nearly launched a nuclear torpedo the same day.
It was the closest the Cold War came to nuclear war, and it reshaped how great powers manage crisis escalation to this day.
1978
Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin were jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for the Camp David negotiations ending their nations' state of war.
The accords behind the prize produced the first peace treaty between Israel and an Arab state, a cornerstone of Middle East diplomacy still in force today.
1991
Turkmenistan declared independence from the Soviet Union after decades as one of its Central Asian republics.
It was one of the final dominoes in the USSR's collapse, and the energy-rich state it created still shapes Central Asian geopolitics today.
