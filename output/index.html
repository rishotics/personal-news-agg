Why it matters

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Angarki Sankashti Chaturthi
Krishna Paksha, Tritiya tithi at sunrise turning to Chaturthi, Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083 — kept as Sankashti Chaturthi because it falls on a Tuesday.

Every month's fourth day of the waning moon, Krishna Paksha Chaturthi, is observed as Sankashti Chaturthi, a fast day set aside for Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles.

Tradition holds that when this Chaturthi lands on a Tuesday, as it does today, it becomes Angarki Chaturthi, named after Angaraka, the fiery planet Mars.

The story goes that Angarak, son of the earth goddess and the sage Bharadwaj, spent years in penance worshipping Ganesha and asking only to be joined with the god's name forever.

Pleased with his devotion, Ganesha appeared before him on a Tuesday and granted that any Sankashti falling on that day would carry Angarak's name and fulfil the wishes of whoever kept its fast.

Devotees still mark the day by fasting from sunrise, breaking the vow only after sighting the moon in the evening and offering prayers to Ganesha.

The day asks worshippers to set aside one obstacle troubling them and lay it, in faith, before the elephant-headed god said to clear every path.
