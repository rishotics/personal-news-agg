🕉 This Day Significance & History

Maha Ashtami (Durga Ashtami)
Shukla Paksha Ashtami tithi (Saptami until 8:28 AM) in the Hindu month of Ashwin, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon stands on Shukla Paksha Ashtami of Ashwin, the eighth day of Sharad Navratri and the third day of Durga Puja, a tithi Hindus call Maha Ashtami.

Tradition tells that as Goddess Durga battled the buffalo-demon Mahishasura and his forces, two of his generals, Chanda and Munda, tried to strike at her by stealth.

Her face darkened with fury at the ambush, and from her own forehead there sprang the terrible, dark form of the goddess Chamunda, armed and ready for slaughter.

Chamunda cut down Chanda and Munda, and in the fighting that followed she also drank the blood of the demon Raktabija so his cloned army could spawn no further.

On this day worshippers also perform Astra Puja, placing flowers and vermilion upon swords, bows, and other weapons in honour of the arms by which good overcame evil.

Maha Ashtami asks those who observe it to face cruelty and betrayal without flinching, and to see their own small courage as a spark of the goddess's own.

1685
King Louis XIV of France revoked the Edict of Nantes, stripping French Protestants of the legal toleration they had held for nearly a century.
The revocation triggered a mass exodus of skilled Huguenots across Europe and is still cited as a foundational case study in religious persecution and the economic cost of intolerance.
1867
The United States formally took possession of Alaska from Russia in a ceremony at Sitka, completing a purchase many Americans had mocked as a folly.
The deal gave the U.S. a resource-rich Arctic and Pacific frontier that later proved strategically vital, from gold and oil to Cold War defense.
1945
The indictment against 24 leading Nazi officials was filed at the first public session of the International Military Tribunal in Berlin, formally launching the Nuremberg process.
It established the first international framework for prosecuting crimes against peace, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, a model still used by international courts today.
