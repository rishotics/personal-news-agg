🕉 This Day Significance & History

Vijayadashami (Dussehra)
Shukla Paksha Navami tithi at sunrise, giving way to Dashami by midday, in the month of Ashwin — the day of Vijayadashami, closing Navratri and Durga Puja.

Today is Vijayadashami, the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Ashwin, which brings Navratri to its close and gives the festival of Dussehra its name.

Tradition holds that it was on this day that Lord Rama, after nine days spent invoking the goddess Durga for strength, finally slew the ten-headed demon king Ravana on the battlefield of Lanka.

Ravana had carried off Rama's wife Sita to his island kingdom, and Rama crossed the sea with an army of Vanaras led by the devoted Hanuman to win her back.

The fall of Ravana is remembered as the victory of dharma over adharma, and across North India towering effigies of Ravana, his brother Kumbhakarna, and his son Meghnad are set ablaze each evening of this day.

In Bengal and much of eastern India the same tithi marks a separate and equally cherished moment, as the clay image of Goddess Durga, who is believed to have descended to her parental home for these nine days, is ceremonially immersed and sent back to Mount Kailash.

The day asks those who observe it to look inward and burn away their own pride, anger, and greed just as the effigy burns outward, choosing the harder road of righteousness over the easier one of ego.
