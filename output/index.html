🕉 This Day Significance & History

Anant Chaturdashi (Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturdashi)
Shukla Paksha Chaturdashi, Bhadrapada month, Vikram Samvat 2083 (tithi runs until 11:06 PM, when Purnima begins)

Today falls the fourteenth day of the waxing moon in Bhadrapada, kept across the country as Anant Chaturdashi in honour of Vishnu's boundless form, Ananta, coiled as the serpent Shesha.

Tradition holds that Krishna came to the forest to console Yudhishthira during the Pandavas' exile after they had lost their kingdom at dice, and when the king poured out his grief, Krishna taught him the Ananta Vrata as the way back to his throne.

Krishna is remembered by the vrat katha as saying, "By performing this Vrat, you will certainly regain your kingdom," and Yudhishthira observed it faithfully with his brothers.

To explain the rite, Krishna is said to have recounted an older tale within the same story, of the sage Kaundinya and his wife Sushila, who found prosperity after tying a sacred fourteen-knotted thread to Ananta, then lost everything when Kaundinya scorned the thread as mere superstition and burned it in pride.

Chastened, Kaundinya wandered in search of the god, was refused by the very things he had once taken for granted, and was finally restored only after years of humble penance and renewed devotion to the thread.

The day asks devotees to bathe, fast, worship Vishnu in his Ananta form, and tie the turmeric-dyed Anant Sutra on the arm, a plain reminder that good fortune is a trust to be honoured with humility, not a possession to be claimed with pride.
