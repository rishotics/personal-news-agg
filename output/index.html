PARSER: Read in Parallel, Reason in Depth for Long-Context LLM Agents

Chinese University of Hong Kong · Kun Li, Zexuan Qiu, Tianhua Zhang, Irwin King, Helen Meng
Why it matters
  1. Decouples document traversal from reasoning depth by reading long-context chunks in parallel rather than sequentially.
  2. Novelty is breaking the standard sequential memory-agent pattern that ties inference latency linearly to document length.
  3. Addresses a documented weakness in long-context agents: sensitivity to where key evidence sits in the document.
  4. Directly relevant to anyone deploying long-context LLM agents for retrieval, QA, or document analysis at scale.
  5. Points toward a shift in agent design away from sequential memory updates and toward parallel-read architectures.

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Amavasya (Pithori Amavasya)
Krishna Paksha Amavasya (new-moon tithi, prevailing till about 8:38 AM) in the Hindu month of Bhadrapada, Vikram Samvat 2083.

Today the moon disappears from the sky, marking Amavasya, the no-moon day that closes the dark fortnight of Bhadrapada and is also known as Pithori Amavasya.

Tradition holds that a grieving mother had lost her children one by one and, desperate with sorrow, prayed for a way to save whichever child remained to her.

The Divine Mother appeared to her and instructed her to worship the sixty-four yoginis, forms of the Goddess Shakti, on this very Amavasya using small figurines shaped from wheat or rice flour, which is why the day takes its name from pithori, the flour used in the ritual.

The mother observed the fast and the worship exactly as told, and her remaining children were restored to health and long life.

Since that day married women across many parts of India observe this vrat, fasting from dawn and shaping the flour idols to seek the same protection for their own sons and daughters.

As the last Amavasya before Pitru Paksha begins, the day also asks households to remember their ancestors, light a lamp at dusk, and offer water and prayers so that the family's bond with those who came before is not forgotten.
