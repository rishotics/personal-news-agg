Learning to Solve Hard Problems in RL for LLMs by Never Giving Up

Mila / Allen Institute for AI · Michael Noukhovitch, Hamish Ivison, Nathan Lambert, Aaron Courville
Why it matters
  1. Identifies a Matthew Effect in RL for LLMs: gains concentrate on easy problems while hard problems barely improve during training
  2. Diagnoses why standard RL with verifiable rewards stalls on the tail of difficult problems that matter most for reasoning capability
  3. Proposes a Never-Give-Up-inspired exploration method that specifically targets hard problems instead of just reweighting easy ones

  5. Reframes the RL-for-LLMs research agenda around exploration and curriculum rather than just reward design or scale

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Bhadrapada Shukla Shashthi (Skanda Shashthi)
Shukla Paksha Panchami until 08:59 AM, then Shukla Paksha Shashthi begins, in the Hindu month of Bhadrapada, Vikram Samvat 2083, Vishakha nakshatra, Wednesday.

As Panchami gives way to Shashthi this morning, the day turns over to Lord Murugan, also known as Skanda or Kartikeya, whose monthly Shashthi is treasured across South India as a day of his triumph over evil.

Tradition tells of the demon Surapadman, who grew so powerful that he tortured the gods, destroyed their sacrifices, and spread fear everywhere.

Unable to bear his tyranny, the devas turned to Lord Shiva, who brought forth Murugan from his own divine fire, a radiant child born with six faces and immense strength.

Grown into a warrior, Murugan took up the Vel, the sacred spear given to him by his mother, and led the army of the gods into a battle against Surapadman that raged for six days.

On the sixth day he struck the demon down, and rather than being destroyed outright, one part of Surapadman became a peacock that became Murugan's vehicle while the other became a rooster on his banner.

The day asks devotees to keep the Shashthi fast and invoke Murugan's courage, remembering that true victory lies in holding fast to dharma against the anger, greed and pride within.
