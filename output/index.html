Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To

Stanford/Oxford · Dianqiao Lei, Kevin Qinghong Lin, Pan Lu, Philip Torr, James Zou
Why it matters
  1. Identifies a systematic failure mode where reasoning LLMs ignore explicit no-thinking instructions and keep generating chain-of-thought
  2. First rigorous study to formally define and measure no-thinking behavior rather than treating it as an afterthought proxy
  3. Finds this inertia persists across prominent reasoning models, revealing a widespread and previously undocumented compliance gap
  4. Matters for anyone deploying reasoning models where latency and cost depend on controllable inference-time behavior
  5. Suggests current instruction-following and RLHF training fail to give users real control over reasoning depth, motivating new training fixes

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Navratri Tritiya – Chandraghanta Puja
Shukla Paksha Tritiya, Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today is the third day of Sharad Navratri, when the bright half of the Hindu month of Ashwin is on its Tritiya tithi, and devotees turn their worship toward Maa Chandraghanta, the third form of the Navadurga.

Tradition holds that the goddess took this fierce and radiant form to destroy the demons who were terrorising the heavens after Mahishasura's rise to power, her forehead crowned with a crescent moon shaped like a bell, which gives her the name Chandraghanta.

She rides a tiger into battle, her ten arms bearing swords, bows, and maces, a warrior goddess whose very appearance strikes fear into the hearts of evildoers while radiating calm toward those who seek her protection.

The legend says that the sound of the bell on her crown is so powerful it can silence the cries of demons and the troubles of ordinary devotees alike, restoring courage where fear had taken hold.

Worshippers light a lamp before her image, offer milk and milk sweets, and chant her name seeking the removal of obstacles, sins, and the evil eye from their lives.

The day asks devotees to cultivate her blend of grace and valour in their own conduct, facing life's battles with composure rather than aggression, and to carry that courage forward into the remaining days of Navratri.
