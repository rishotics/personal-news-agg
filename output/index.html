Safety

Is Deep Research Reliable? Misleading Knowledge Induces False Conclusions

Independent/Academic (China) · Pengyu Zhu, Lijun Li, Longju Yang, Sen Su
Why it matters
  1. Systematically probes whether Deep Research agents, now widely deployed for long-horizon planning, retrieval, and report generation, can be corrupted by misleading but plausible-looking web content.
  2. Novel angle: rather than testing factual accuracy alone, it injects credible-seeming misleading knowledge into open information environments and traces how it propagates into final conclusions.
  3. Demonstrates a concrete failure mode where deep research pipelines confidently synthesize false conclusions from poisoned evidence, undermining trust in their outputs.
  4. Directly relevant to every lab and product shipping agentic research assistants, including OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic deep research features used by millions.
  5. Raises the urgent question of how retrieval-augmented reasoning systems should verify provenance before autonomous agents are trusted for high-stakes decision support.
