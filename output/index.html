🕉 This Day Significance & History

Avidhava Navami (Navami Shraddha)
Krishna Paksha Navami tithi, Ashwin (Ashwina) month, Punarvasu nakshatra, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today the moon stands on Navami, the ninth day of the waning fortnight of Ashwin, placing the date deep within Pitru Paksha, the fortnight set aside for the ancestors.

Hindu tradition holds that the souls of three generations of forebears dwell in Pitriloka, a realm between heaven and earth that is ruled over by Yama, the god of death.

This particular Navami is called Avidhava Navami, a day kept not for forefathers in general but for the mothers, grandmothers and great-grandmothers of the family who died as married women, before their husbands.

Custom asks that the eldest son perform the rite, inviting only his mother, his maternal grandmother and his paternal great-grandmother, setting this shraddha apart from the wider rites that summon every ancestor together.

Tarpan and pinda daan are offered along with a feast for married women and Brahmins, while households also turn in reverence to the deities called Dhurilochan, said to keep their eyes half closed against the smoke of the sacred fires.

The day asks families to pause and settle a debt tradition says can never be fully repaid, offering food, cloth and quiet prayer so that the women who gave the family its life may find peace.
