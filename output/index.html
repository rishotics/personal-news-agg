🕉 This Day Significance & History

Papankusha Ekadashi
Shukla Paksha Ekadashi, Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083 (Nakshatra Shatabhisha)

Today the moon moves through the eleventh tithi of the bright fortnight of Ashwin, a day known across the tradition as Papankusha Ekadashi, dedicated to Lord Vishnu in his form as Padmanabha.

The word itself means the goad against sin, papa being sin and ankusha the hook used to turn back a wild elephant, and the day is said to turn back even the heaviest misdeeds of a lifetime.

The katha is told by Krishna to Yudhishthira in the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, about a cruel hunter named Krodhana who lived in the Vindhya mountains and spent his years in violence and wrongdoing.

When Krodhana grew old and the messengers of Yama approached, terror of death sent him running to the sage Angira, who counselled him to observe this single Ekadashi with full faith.

The hunter fasted, stayed awake through the night in worship of Vishnu, and by the merit of that one vow his sins were pierced away as the ankusha controls an elephant, carrying him at last to Vaikuntha.

The day asks its observers to fast, to keep the mind on Vishnu rather than on idle talk, and to remember that sincere repentance, however late in life, can still open the gate to grace.
