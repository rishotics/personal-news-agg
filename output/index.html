🤖 Paper of the Day AI & Robotics Research

ML Theory

The Lattice of Transition Laws

Apple (Jiatao Gu) · T. Y. Tsui, Jiatao Gu, Lingjie Liu
Why it matters
  1. Proposes a unifying framework for diffusion and autoregressive generative models via a lattice of transition laws governing decoding order and schedule
  2. Novelty lies in showing fixed hybrid decoding schedules are a special case of a broader combinatorial space of transition laws, not two separate paradigms
  3. Unifying diffusion and AR under one theoretical lattice gives a principled way to derive new hybrid schedules rather than hand-tuning them
  4. Affects researchers building next-generation generative models for both continuous data like images and discrete tokens like text
  5. Opens the door to systematically searching the lattice for better speed-quality tradeoffs instead of ad hoc hybrid designs

🕉 This Day Significance & History

Sharad Navratri Begins — Shukla Pratipada
Shukla Paksha Pratipada, Ashwin month, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today is Shukla Pratipada of the Ashwin month, the first day of the nine-night festival of Sharad Navratri, and devotees across India perform Ghatasthapana by installing a sacred kalash to invoke the Goddess into their homes.

This opening day is dedicated to Maa Shailputri, the Daughter of the Mountains, who is worshipped as the first of the nine forms of Durga.

Tradition tells that she was once Sati, daughter of King Daksha, who gave up her own life in the sacrificial fire after her father insulted her husband Shiva by refusing him an invitation to a grand yagna.

Unable to bear the dishonour done to Shiva, Sati renounced her body, and the devastated Shiva carried her remains across the heavens in grief until Vishnu's discus cut them apart, scattering them as the Shakti Peethas revered to this day.

She was reborn as Parvati, daughter of Himavan the lord of the Himalayas, and as Shailputri she undertook fierce penance to win Shiva back as her husband, proving that devotion can reunite what sorrow once tore apart.

The day asks worshippers to begin their nine-day vow with purity of intent, tending the sown barley in the kalash as a living symbol of the patience and faith that the Goddess's long penance once demanded.
