🕉 This Day Significance & History

Nag Panchami (Krishna Panchami)
Krishna Paksha Chaturthi till dawn, giving way to Panchami, in the month of Bhadrapada, Vikram Samvat 2083

Today's tithi is Panchami of the dark fortnight of Bhadrapada, and in many households and temples this is kept as Nag Panchami, the day set aside for the serpent deities.

Tradition traces the observance to King Janamejaya, great-grandson of Arjuna, who launched the terrible Sarpa Satra, a sacrificial fire meant to wipe out every serpent on earth after his father Parikshit died from the bite of Takshaka, king of the Nagas.

As the chants of the officiating priests grew ever more powerful, serpents from every corner of creation were pulled helplessly into the flames, and Takshaka fled to the heavens and coiled around the throne of Indra for shelter.

So fierce was the ritual that even Indra began to be dragged toward the fire, and the frightened gods turned for help to Manasa Devi, the goddess of serpents.

She sent her young and wise son Astika to the sacrificial hall, and his eloquence so pleased Janamejaya that the king offered him any boon he wished, whereupon Astika asked only that the slaughter be stopped, sparing Takshaka and the remaining Naga race.

The day asks those who observe it to set aside fear of the serpent, to offer milk and prayer at anthills or serpent images, and to remember that wisdom spoken with courage once stayed the hand of a king bent on vengeance.
